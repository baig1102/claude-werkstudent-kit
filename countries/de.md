# Germany (DE): work rules for students

**Not legal advice.** Checked on 16 November 2026. Rules and amounts change, and your own residence permit can say something different. What counts is the law, the text on your permit and its supplementary sheet (Zusatzblatt), and the answer of your local Ausländerbehörde. If you are unsure, ask your university's International Office first.

## 1. Who has a limit
- **Students from EU/EEA countries and Switzerland:** free access to the German labour market, like German students. [DAAD]
- **Students from other countries with a residence permit for study (§ 16b AufenthG):** the limits in section 2 apply. [Law] [MiiG]

## 2. The residence-law limit (non-EU students)
- You may work **140 full days or 280 half days per year**. A day with up to four hours of work counts as a half day. [MiiG] [DAAD]
- **Alternative weekly counting:** up to **20 hours per week during the lecture period**. In the law, such a week counts as 2.5 working days on your "working-day account", and you may choose the more favourable counting method week by week. The paragraph also covers weeks outside the lecture period; read the law text for the details. [Law] [MiiG]
- **Student assistant jobs** at the university (studentische Nebentätigkeiten, for example HiWi) do not count towards the 140 days. DAAD says you must still inform the Ausländerbehörde. [Law] [DAAD]
- **Self-employed or freelance work** needs the approval of the Ausländerbehörde first. [MiiG] [DAAD]
- **More than the limit** needs the consent of the Ausländerbehörde. [DAAD]
- **Internships:** university advice pages state that voluntary internships count towards the 140 days and mandatory internships that are part of the degree do not. Confirm this for your case. [Uni-HD]
- **Language-course and preparatory-course students:** other conditions can apply. Ask before you take a job. [Law]

## 3. The social-insurance rule (all students, any nationality)
This is a second, separate "20-hour rule". It does not decide whether you may work. It decides which contributions are taken from your pay.
- As a Werkstudent you keep the student status in health, long-term-care and unemployment insurance only if you work **no more than 20 hours per week**. Pension insurance contributions are due. [MJZ]
- **All your jobs are added together** for the 20 hours. [MJZ] [TK]
- Exceptions exist for work in the evening, at night, at weekends and in the semester break, if it is limited in advance, for at most 26 weeks per year. [MJZ]

## 4. Amounts in 2026
- Legal minimum wage: **€13.90 per hour** since 1 January 2026. [MJZ-2026]
- Minijob limit: **€603 per month** on average. [MJZ-2026]
- Both are set to change on 1 January 2027. Check the Minijob-Zentrale page before you rely on a number.

## 5. What this means for the kit
- Put your weekly limit into `{{PERMIT_HOURS}}`. For most non-EU students in the lecture period this is 20, all jobs together.
- If you count days and not weeks, keep a simple list of working days. The tracker does not do this for you.
- A second job never adds hours in the lecture period. The skills treat the limit as one total.
- A HiWi job at your university can sit outside the 140 days, but the social-insurance rule in section 3 still counts its hours.

## 6. Words you will see in postings
| German | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Werkstudent / Werkstudentin, Working Student | Enrolled student working part-time, usually up to 20 h/week |
| Studentische Hilfskraft (SHK), HiWi, WHK | Student assistant at a university or research institute |
| Minijob, Aushilfe, geringfügige Beschäftigung | Small job up to the Minijob limit |
| Pflichtpraktikum / freiwilliges Praktikum | Mandatory / voluntary internship |
| Immatrikulationsbescheinigung | Enrolment certificate (almost always required) |
| Anschreiben, Lebenslauf, Zeugnisse | Cover letter, CV, certificates |
| "bereits besetzt", "nicht mehr verfügbar", "inaktiv" | The posting is closed |
| "sehr gute / fließende / verhandlungssichere Deutschkenntnisse" | Roughly C1 or higher is expected |

## 7. Application customs (typical, not rules)
- The CV (Lebenslauf) is a table with dates, newest first, one to two pages. A photo is common and optional.
- Certificates are sent as separate PDF files unless the portal asks for one file.
- Many portals have no field for a cover letter. Then none is needed.
